## 2. The flagged characters: made rules legal, the player told what changed (done 2026-11-23)

Built: src/aurora/makeLegal.ts, src/ui/MakeLegal.tsx, `acceptedFindings` on the character, the Journal entry "Rules
changes". Only leftover picks are done without asking; every other finding is a call. Not built: "Replace with a Bard
cantrip" as its own button (taking the pick out reopens the choice on Build) and the point-buy call (it is a note,
not a finding). Found on the way: two weapons and a shield was not counted as too much to hold.

One screen, "Make it legal", opened from the rules check when it has findings (and from the older-build notice).
It works on a copy first and shows before anything is saved:

- **Done for you** (one safe answer, the fix-it machinery of E3.1, src/aurora/findingFix.ts): a repeated pick taken
  out, a pick its choice no longer offers taken out, too many picks trimmed, leftover picks cleared.
- **Your call**, each with its options as buttons, nothing chosen for the player:
  - A 2024 species with a 2014 background and no increases (Talaan Istodaewl, Indigo Bones, Waldrom Brahms, Drago):
    "Use the 2024 rules for this background (three points, an origin feat)" or "Take a 2024 background" or "Leave it".
  - Cantrips off the class list (Carl Sneakers: Shape Water and Spare the Dying as Bard cantrips): "Replace with a
    Bard cantrip" (opens that choice) or "Keep as a table ruling" (marks the finding as accepted, see below).
  - More held than two hands hold (Xarthian): which items to put away, as a list to tick.
  - Point buy unspent (item 1): "Spend them" (opens the scores) or "Leave them".
- **Accepted as a table ruling**: a finding the player keeps on purpose is remembered on the character
  (`acceptedFindings: string[]`, rule plus detail) and shown apart, greyed, never counted as a fault again.
- **What changed**, as a list in plain words, with one button: "Add to this character's notes". It goes into a
  "Rules changes" entry of the Journal (src/ui/Journal.tsx; the journal already lives with the character and exports
  with it), dated. The import and move notes (`importReport`) can be added the same way, so all such notes have one
  home. Open question for Chris: Journal (proposed) or the Bag of Holding; the Journal needs no new place.
- The original is never overwritten silently: Apply writes the changes; Cancel leaves the character as it was.
- Tests: findingFix.test.ts for each automatic fix; a test per decision that each option leads to a character with
  that finding gone (or accepted); an end-to-end test of the screen on a seeded flagged character.

## 3. The shop: the one place items come from (done 2026-11-23)

Built as below, with two choices made on the way: the Buy/Add switch is kept for the app (keptFilters), not per
character; a shelf shows only its own kind and keeps its own filters ("Everything" is the unfiltered list). The
converter now carries each 5e.tools item's kind (it filed every mundane item as "Adventuring Gear").

- The Shop tab becomes the only way to add items, besides starting equipment. Removed: the "All items" list on the
  board's equipment panel and the adding part of EquipmentTab (BuildBoard.tsx two `<Browse type="Item">`, 
  EquipmentTab `part="adding"`); the custom-item form moves into the shop ("Something not on the shelves").
- A switch at the top: **Buy** (takes gold, the price shown, refused when the purse is short) or **Add** (free).
  Kept per character (`shopMode`), default Buy when the character has started play, Add while building.
- A shopkeeper: a name, a line of greeting that changes with the purse and the last purchase, at the head of the
  tab. No generated art (the standing rule): the keeper is words and the existing icon set; if Chris wants a
  drawn keeper, he supplies or approves the art.
- Categories first: a row of large tiles (Weapons, Armor and shields, Adventuring gear, Tools, Packs, Potions and
  poisons, Mounts and vehicles, Magic items, Trinkets, Everything). A tile opens the list with that category's
  filter set, and the filters stay the player's to change (the kept filters, item 4).
- The category of an item is one function used by the tiles, the list's badge and the bag (today `itemBadge` in
  Browse.tsx and the bag's own grouping): one source.
- Tests: shop.test.ts (buy takes gold, add does not, short purse refused, sell-back unchanged); an end-to-end
  test: no way to add an item outside the shop except starting equipment; a tile opens its filtered list.

## 5b. A guided first character, as part of the tutorial (Chris, same day)

**First version done 2026-11-23** (src/aurora/guide.ts, src/ui/GuideCoach.tsx, the tutorial's first screen,
e2e/guide.spec.ts). Built: the offer, two questions, class, species and background recommendations, the recommended
scores, the count of open choices, the end. Not built yet: marks on recommended options inside the lists; a way to
start the guide outside the tutorial; hiding non-SRD content while the guide runs; the tutorial's own spotlight on
the list to open. The questions and the table's wording are for Chris to review.

- First launch asks two things before anything else: "New to D&D?" and "Shall I guide you through making your first
  character?" (Yes, No, Later; the answer is kept, and Settings can start the guide again).
- The guide is the tutorial (src/ui/Tutorial.tsx), not a second wizard: the player does the real things on the real
  board, with the guide pointing at each (the tutorial's spotlight) and waiting for it to be done. Nothing is done
  for them.
- It asks plain questions and turns the answers into recommendations, never into picks:
  - "What sounds like fun: hitting things, casting spells, sneaking and talking, healing and protecting?" leads to
    two or three classes, each with one line on how it plays.
  - "How much do you want to keep track of?" (little, some, a lot) orders them (a Champion Fighter before a Wizard).
  - Then species ("who are your people?") and background ("what did you do before?"), the same way.
  - Scores: "use the recommended spread" as one click (the standard array placed for the class), or do it by hand.
- Each step names the action: "Drag the Fighter card onto the Class space" (or click it), then shows what the card
  gave and what it still asks. Recommended options carry a mark in the lists (the lists' "Recommended" order
  already exists); the rest stay available.
- A new player sees the free SRD rules and the 2024 edition only during the guide, so the lists are short.
- Where recommendations come from: a small table in the app (class by play style and complexity, a species and a
  background that suit each class), written by hand and reviewed by Chris, no generated text at run time.
- It ends with a finished, rules-legal level 1 character, the sheet shown, and "what next" (level up, the shop,
  Play). Leaving midway keeps the character and offers to carry on later.
- Tests: the recommendation table (every recommended class, species and background exists in the SRD bundle; every
  answer leads somewhere); an end-to-end test that answers the questions, follows each instruction and ends with a
  character the rules check passes.
- Open questions for Chris: the exact questions and their wording; whether the guide may run on 5e.tools content
  when that is loaded, or always on the SRD.
